What "1–6 months" actually means
It means you are not making just one forecast. You are testing your models at multiple forecast distances into the future, specifically:

1 month ahead
2 months ahead
3 months ahead
4 months ahead
5 months ahead
6 months ahead

So if your data ends in December 2023, your model would try to predict January 2024 (1 month ahead), February 2024 (2 months ahead), all the way to June 2024 (6 months ahead) — and you check how accurate each of those predictions turned out to be.

Why test multiple horizons instead of just one?
Because forecast accuracy is not constant — it changes the further into the future you try to predict.
Think of it like weather forecasting: tomorrow's forecast is usually quite accurate. A forecast for 6 days from now is shakier. The same logic applies to fuel prices.
So your project is asking:

"How far into the future can these models still be trusted?"


How Objective 4 and Objective 5 differ
Objective 4 — the statistical question:

"To compare the forecasting accuracy of the three models across 1–6 month forecast horizons using RMSE, MAE, MAPE, and the Diebold-Mariano test."

This is purely about numbers. You compute RMSE, MAE, MAPE at each horizon (1 month, 2 months... 6 months) for each model (ARIMA, ARIMAX, VAR), then use the Diebold-Mariano test to check if differences between models are statistically significant. The output here is a table of error scores.

Objective 5 — the practical/policy question:

"To evaluate the practical utility of the models in providing 1–6 month advance warning of fuel price changes to consumers and policymakers in Ghana."

This goes one step further than just numbers. It asks: "Given those accuracy results, is this actually useful for real people?"
For example, you might find:

ARIMAX is highly accurate at 1–2 months but becomes unreliable by month 4
VAR stays reasonably accurate even at 6 months

Objective 5 is where you translate that into a real-world statement like:

"The findings suggest that policymakers can reliably use the ARIMAX model for short-term (1–2 month) planning, but should rely on the VAR model for longer-term (up to 6 month) advance warning."
